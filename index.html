India GDP Explainer

India's GDP base moved to 2022–23.

The base year moved from 2011–12 to 2022–23. Methods were updated, new data was added, and past growth rates were revised down.

Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) · 27 Feb 2026 · PIB release PRID 2233518

The takeaway

India's economy is measured against a newer, more representative picture of the economy — but the new picture shows slightly slower growth than previously reported.

Real growth was revised from 9.2% to 7.2% for one recent year, and from 6.5% to 7.1% for another. Nominal GDP levels were marked down 2.9% in 2022–23, and 3.8% in each of 2023–24 and 2024–25. The level markdowns are the government's own figures, as reported in The Diplomat.

Why it's better

  • Newer base. 2022–23 replaces 2011–12, so weights reflect how India produces and spends today.
  • Quarterly method upgraded. Denton benchmarking aligns quarterly estimates with annual totals.
  • Manufacturing deflation fixed. Double deflation separates input and output prices instead of using a single deflator.
  • New data sources. GST records, MCA corporate filings, the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES), PLFS labour data, ASUSE services survey, ASI industry data, e-Vahan vehicle registrations, and PFMS spending data.

The controversy

The case for the revision

  • An 11-year-old base mismeasures a changed economy.
  • Administrative data (GST, MCA, PFMS) is more complete than old surveys.
  • Double deflation is standard international practice.

The sceptics' case

  • Large back-revisions cut nominal GDP by nearly 4% — the denominator for deficit and debt ratios moves.
  • Base choice matters: 2022–23 at ₹80.00 lakh crore vs. ₹88.27 lakh crore (government) vs. ₹86.05 lakh crore (Garg) for recent output.
  • Timing invites questions: revisions land alongside strong headlines — Q1 FY27 real growth of 7.8% (HT, 3 Sep 2026) and FY26 SAE of 7.6% GDP / 7.7% GVA (MoSPI).

What to watch

  • Back-series. How far back the revised history goes, and which years move most.
  • Deflators. Whether double deflation changes the manufacturing story quarter to quarter.
  • Ratios. Fiscal deficit, debt, and tax-to-GDP recomputed on the lower nominal base.
  • Q1 FY27. 7.8% real growth print (HT, 3 Sep 2026) — how it reads on the new base.
  • FY26 SAE. 7.6% GDP and 7.7% GVA second advance estimates (MoSPI).
